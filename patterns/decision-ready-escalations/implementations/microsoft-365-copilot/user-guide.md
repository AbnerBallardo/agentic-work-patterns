# Use the Agent to Prepare an Escalation

- **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-11
- **Audience:** People preparing a decision, escalation, or executive communication

## What the Agent Does

The agent helps you understand the issue before it helps you write the message. It can expose missing context, distinguish facts from assumptions, clarify the requested decision, and compress the result into a brief.

It does not verify your facts, own your recommendation, choose the final channel, send the message, or make the decision.

## Access Through the Team

If your organization distributes the agent through a Microsoft Team, use the agent link published in the Team's main channel or guidance. The link may open the agent in Microsoft 365 Copilot rather than inside the Teams conversation.

Team membership, agent-sharing policy, and Microsoft 365 licensing can all affect access. If the agent does not open, confirm those conditions with the Team or agent owner instead of requesting a separate copy of the agent.

## Start With the Minimum Safe Context

Do not begin by pasting an entire email chain. Provide only information approved for use in Microsoft 365 Copilot and necessary for the decision.

Use this starting structure when helpful:

```text
What changed:
Why it matters now:
Decision or action needed:
Known facts:
Assumptions or unknowns:
Viable options:
My recommendation:
Material risks or trade-offs:
Next owner and deadline:
```

Then ask:

```text
Help me determine whether this is ready for escalation. Ask only questions that block a safe and useful brief. Do not invent missing facts or a recommendation for me.
```

## Respond to the Readiness Result

| Result | What you should do |
|---|---|
| Ready | Validate every fact, risk, owner, date, and recommendation before using the draft. |
| Needs information | Answer the blocking questions or identify the person who can. |
| Human review required | Involve the appropriate accountable, legal, risk, security, financial, personnel, or communications owner. |
| Use another channel | Speak with the relevant people before creating or sending the written record. |

## Use the Draft Responsibly

Before sending anything:

1. Confirm that the requested decision belongs to the recipient.
2. Verify the facts against their sources.
3. Make sure the recommendation is yours.
4. Restore any risk, uncertainty, or disagreement lost during compression.
5. Check that the owner and timing are realistic.
6. Decide whether email is still the right channel.
7. Edit the language so it sounds like you and fits the relationship.

The output is preparation material, not permission to escalate.

## When Someone Approaches a Leader Directly

For a routine, non-urgent issue, the leader can ask:

> Have you validated the issue with the Decision-Ready Escalations agent? If not, use it to clarify the decision, recommendation, and missing context, then come back to me or send the brief.

This should be a coaching prompt, not an inflexible gate. If the matter is genuinely urgent, sensitive, or unsafe to enter into the system, address it immediately. Afterward, explain how the agent could help prepare a similar issue next time when circumstances allow.

The objective is to improve thinking and communication habits—not to prevent access to leadership.

## Protect Sensitive Information

- Follow your organization's approved-use and data-classification rules.
- Do not enter secrets, credentials, unnecessary personal data, or restricted material.
- Remember that removing names may not make a situation anonymous.
- When uncertain, provide a sanitized description or use a human channel instead.
