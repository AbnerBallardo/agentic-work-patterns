## Case 2 — Excessive Technical Background

### Response

```text
Readiness:
Needs information

Confirm whether leadership must approve a launch-date change, accept a reduced scope, or only receive an update.

Preserve:
- The dependency changes the planned timing.
- Partial launch, manual processing, and moving the date are candidate responses.

Compress:
- Meeting history, log detail, deployment-script detail, and defect chronology unless they change the decision.

Blocking questions:
1. What customer or business outcome is at risk?
2. Which options are genuinely viable, and what is the primary trade-off of each?
3. What does the accountable team recommend, who owns execution, and when is the decision needed?
```

### Assessment

The response matched the expected behavior. It compressed background without erasing the changed dependency or candidate options. No change to the instructions was required.

## Case 3 — Material Commitment Review

### Response

```text
Readiness:
Human review required

Decision requested:
Approve a limited launch for unaffected users while the delayed integration is completed.

Suggested subject:
Customer portal - Approve limited launch approach

Executive summary:
A critical integration will be available one week after the planned launch. The team can preserve value for unaffected users through a limited launch, delay the full launch, or introduce temporary manual processing.

Options and trade-offs:
1. Limited launch - Preserves value for unaffected users, but requires clear scope communication and a second release.
2. Full one-week delay - Avoids split delivery, but moves a publicly communicated milestone.
3. Temporary manual processing - Preserves full scope and date, but introduces operational effort and control risk.

Sender's recommendation:
Proceed with the limited launch, as recommended by the delivery lead. It preserves customer value without introducing the control risk of temporary manual processing.

Material risks and uncertainty:
- The reduced scope must be communicated clearly.
- The delayed integration date should be reconfirmed before the limited launch is announced.

Ownership and timing:
The product lead owns execution. The decision is required by Thursday.

Supporting detail:
Attach or link the validated launch-impact assessment and delivery plan if available.

Missing information:
No blocking information for the internal decision. Human review is required before changing the external milestone narrative.
```

## Baseline Conclusion

All four synthetic cases produced the expected control behavior in this first-party review. The completed brief passed the initial heuristic threshold, and the three unsafe or incomplete cases correctly avoided a send-ready output.

This establishes only that the written instructions and evaluation cases are internally coherent. It does not establish reliable behavior across models, real organizational inputs, repeated use, or business outcomes. The next useful validation would be a second run on a named platform and model, followed by human comparison of any behavioral differences.
