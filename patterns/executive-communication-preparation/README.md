# Executive Communication Preparation

Help a team prepare onward communication using a leader's guidance about its recipients.

## The Problem

A team can understand an issue and still miss what an executive needs to know. The manager then repeatedly supplies the same context, explains recipient expectations, and reviews similar drafts.

This pattern is for leaders who can make that guidance explicit and teams preparing communication within their direction.

## The Idea in Practice

I used an internal agent called Executive Drafter after my team had brought me the context for a decision or acknowledgment. I could direct them to prepare an escalation to the local CEO or regional CIO, and choose whether they could send after their own review or should return to me first.

For these particular recipients, the CEO needed local business consequences and a clear intervention; the regional CIO also needed local technical dependencies and the wider picture. I maintained separate profiles from what I learned in our interactions. The agent helped the team ask better questions and prepare wording and evidence aligned with that guidance.

This is a qualitative account of useful practice, not measured savings. The public materials are fictional reconstructions; the original agent's platform is unspecified. Committees and presentations are possible future extensions, not demonstrated experience.

## How It Works

1. The leader supplies the intended recipient, purpose, current decision or acknowledgment, constraints, and whether the team may send or must return for review.
2. The team provides the recipient's complete current guidance and the case facts, evidence, uncertainty, ownership, and timing.
3. The assistant identifies missing preparation and drafts for that recipient without changing material facts or hiding responsibility.
4. The sender validates the result and completes any required review. The human sends.

The leader can also use a separate conversation to propose profile improvements. Only the guidance owner adopts them; a team chat does not automatically change shared guidance. A profile describes communication needs, not current project facts or sending permission.

This is a configured conversational assistant; no tool is required. It does not send, contact others, change files, or verify source truth. [Decision-Ready Escalations](../decision-ready-escalations/) can supply the initial brief, but a recommendation is not a human decision or onward authorization. See the [handoff example](examples/pattern-handoff.md).

## Try It

1. Read [one situation prepared for two recipients](examples/recipient-specific-preparation.md).
2. Copy the [agent instructions](agent-instructions.md) into your chosen assistant.
3. Supply the fictional inputs, including the profile and leader direction, without the expected answer.
4. Look for different explanations with consistent facts, honest uncertainty, and the correct human review route.

Use the [small profile template](recipient-profile-template.md) to make your own fictional case. If you use M365 Copilot, the [setup suggestion](implementations/microsoft-365-copilot/README.md) provides a short path using profile text in chat. SharePoint is optional.

## Boundaries and Evidence

Do not invent facts, infer authority, or conceal material information even when asked by a leader or an approved profile. Ask when evidence, guidance, or direction is missing or disputed. Keep required reviews visible; return material changes to the leader. The sender reviews and sends within actual permission. Use a different channel when the situation requires it.
