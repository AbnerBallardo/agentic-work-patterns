# Example: Human Handoff Between the Two Patterns

All records, roles, decisions, and messages in this scenario are fictional. Expected responses illustrate how the specifications should work together; this is not a recorded behavioral evaluation or evidence that the combined public workflow has been tested.

## Inputs

### First-Stage Case Material

A team prepares an escalation to its technology director using Decision-Ready Escalations:

- Fictional delivery record R1, reviewed today: a critical integration is forecast to arrive one week after the planned customer portal launch. The supplier date still needs reconfirmation before launch communication.
- Fictional scope assessment R2, reviewed today: unaffected services can launch separately. A limited launch preserves value for those users but needs clear scope communication and a second release.
- Fictional options assessment R3, reviewed today: delaying the entire launch avoids a split release but moves a public milestone. Temporary manual processing preserves scope and timing but introduces control risk.
- The delivery lead recommends the limited launch. The product lead owns execution; the technology director's decision is needed by Thursday.
- No external communication or change to a public commitment has been approved.

### Actual Human Direction, Supplied After First-Stage Review

The technology director reviews the brief and supplies this new instruction:

> Approve planning for the limited launch. Prepare an awareness email to the local CEO explaining the business consequence, remaining supplier uncertainty, and outstanding external-communication approval. Request no further decision or budget commitment. Bring the draft back to me before sending. Return to me if the supplier timing or launch scope changes materially.

Direction record: technology director; issued 2026-10-16; fictional approved review session DIR-PORTAL-16. Supplied directly by the leader in the scenario, covering limited-launch planning and this internal CEO awareness email. Leader review remains required before sending. Changed supplier timing or scope requires fresh leader review before finalization. No external announcement or budget authority is granted.

This instruction is an input from the human leader, not a conclusion generated by either agent. The limited-launch recommendation did not approve itself. The record provides traceability, not independent authentication by the agent.

### Second-Stage Recipient Guidance

Use a separately supplied fictional local CEO profile, approved by the technology director and within its review period. The CEO knows the portal's business purpose but not the integration dependency. Explain available customer value, effects on the public milestone, unresolved risk, and ownership. Include enough dependency context to make those consequences understandable.

The technology director's familiarity and any first-stage access permissions do not establish the CEO's familiarity or access.

## Expected Response

### Decision-Ready Escalations

```text
Readiness: Human review required because the options affect launch scope
or a publicly communicated milestone.

Decision requested: Approve limited-launch planning, delay the full launch,
or select another confirmed viable option.

Executive summary: The integration is forecast one week after the planned
launch. Unaffected services can launch separately; the supplier timing still
needs reconfirmation before launch communication.

Options and trade-offs:
1. Limited launch: preserves value for unaffected users but needs clear scope
   communication and a second release.
2. Full delay: avoids a split release but moves the public milestone.
3. Temporary manual processing: preserves scope and timing but adds control risk.

Sender's recommendation: The delivery lead recommends the limited launch.
Ownership and timing: Product lead owns execution; decision needed Thursday.
Supporting evidence: Supplied fictional records R1, R2, and R3, reviewed today.
Material uncertainty: Supplier timing is not yet reconfirmed.

Next human step: The technology director reviews and decides. No approved
outcome or onward sending permission can be inferred from this draft.
```

### Executive Communication Preparation

After the human direction is supplied, reuse R1–R3 and their uncertainty, alongside the new recipient guidance. Do not treat the first-stage readiness label as proof of factual verification or send authority.

```text
Preparation record
Readiness: Human review required.
Blocking information: None for this truthful review draft.
Required human reviews: Technology director before sending.
Recipient: Local CEO; separately supplied complete current approved profile.
Purpose: Awareness; no further decision requested.
Leader direction: Limited-launch planning approved; no external announcement
or budget commitment authorized. Leader review required before sending.
Direction source: Technology director; 2026-10-16; DIR-PORTAL-16;
direct leader instruction for this portal case and internal CEO email only.
Evidence: R1–R3 supplied by the team, reviewed today.
Uncertainty: Supplier timing still needs reconfirmation.
Return condition: Material change in supplier timing or launch scope.
Next step: Sender validates the draft and returns it to the technology director.

Recipient-facing working draft
Subject: Portal launch planning — limited scope and remaining approvals

We are planning a limited portal launch for unaffected services following
the technology director's review. This approach would preserve value for
those users while the remaining integration is completed, with a second
release and clear communication of the reduced initial scope.

The integration is forecast one week after the planned launch; supplier
timing still needs reconfirmation. The plan affects the scope of a publicly
communicated milestone. External communication has not been approved.

This is for awareness; no further decision is requested here. The product
lead owns execution planning. We will return for review if supplier timing
or launch scope changes materially.
```

This internal draft remains subject to the leader's reserved review. It does not authorize an external announcement, and it does not establish a send date or promise an update date that the inputs did not provide.

### Variants

- **Leader instruction omitted:** Use Needs information and separately record any required human review. The second pattern asks for the purpose, actual decision or acknowledgment, recipient, constraints, and review route. It cannot infer these from the first-stage recommendation.
- **Permission merely asserted or source belongs to another case:** Request the actual applicable leader instruction and human confirmation. Do not infer authority from a reference code alone.
- **Supplier forecast changes to two weeks:** Return the change to the leader before finalizing and preserve uncertainty. Use Needs information when required facts or revised authority are missing, and retain the leader review; use Human review required when the review draft has sufficient supported context and only approval remains. It cannot reinterpret approval for the original plan as approval for the changed situation.
- **Leader later permits direct sending:** Record the new instruction's issuer, date, source, applicable scope, and confirmation status. The sender still reviews the facts and message. Other required approvals, including external-communication approval, remain in force.
- **Awareness-only starting point:** If the leader already has the context and directs an awareness message, the second pattern can start there. No fictional approval request or mandatory first-stage brief is needed.

## What to Evaluate

- The first-stage output preserves options and recommendation ownership while leaving the decision to the human recipient.
- The leader supplies a distinct decision and onward instruction; neither agent fabricates it.
- Evidence, source dates, unresolved uncertainty, and ownership survive the handoff.
- The second pattern changes audience emphasis without claiming a public announcement is approved or a supplier date is confirmed.
- Leader review and return conditions remain explicit even after a complete draft exists.
- The scenario is documented as an expected combined workflow, not a new passing baseline.
